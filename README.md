# Traditional Preservation (Fish & Meat) — Build 42 prototype (v0.4.87)

**Status: experimental.** The stations build, open and hang fish and meat in the game, but a full year hasn't been played. Back up your save before enabling.

Preserves fish and meat the old way, with no power: salt, wood, fat, smoke and the weather. One workshop serves both: the same Salting Tub cures fish and meat, the same drying stations hang both, the Smokehouse smokes both, and butchering gives the fat that seals meat in a ceramic jar. The player guide is `1_FishMeat_Guide.md` in this folder.

## History

The salt route is the lower Volga's: around Astrakhan, fish is salted in vats under a weighted lid, soaked, and dried in the shade, small fish such as vobla whole with their heads on and large ones such as pike split or hung from hooks. Stockfish is unsalted fish hung on open racks through the cold northern Norwegian spring, matured in airy warehouses and traded across Europe since the Viking age. Charque comes from the *charqueadas* of southern Brazil: beef salted in piles, washed and dried in the summer sun, always soaked before it is cooked. Potted Meat is Turkish *kavurma*, French *confit* and the Balkan *meso u masti*: meat cooked in fat and sealed in clay for the winter. The Smokehouse is the Kentucky farm smokehouse and the Bosnian *pušnica*, fed from a low firebox outside the wall; hot smoking follows the Volga's hot smoked fish.

## Install

Copy this folder to `~/Zomboid/mods/TraditionalPreservationFishMeat`, as a real copy rather than a symlink. It needs the **TP Core** mod (`TraditionalPreservationCore`, the series' shared mod) installed and enabled the same way, and no other mods.

## How it works

### Preparing fish and meat

- **Gut Fish** (a raw whole fish, a sharp knife or meat cleaver kept): a Gutted Fish, which keeps like fresh fish and must be cooked like one, plus Fish Guts and a Fish Head if it has them, both as old as the fish. Fillets can't be gutted.
- **Split Fish**: a Gutted Fish over 2.5 weight, too heavy for a frame, becomes two Fish Halves, each half the weight and food, named for the fish (*Medium Walleye Half*). A gutted fish at a later stage halves at that stage: a **Salt Cured** one over 2.5 kg (so it hangs on a frame), a **Salt Dried** one or a **Stockfish** over 0.6 kg (*Medium Walleye Half (Salt Cured)*); each half keeps its stage's marks (rinsed, maturing). A small fish salted whole with its guts, a half and a fillet don't split (`F.canSplit`).
- **Slice Fish**: a whole fish over 0.6 weight can still be filleted after it's been salted, dried or rehydrated: 2 fillets at the same stage, each with half the fish's food and weight (a cured fish has no guts left to lose). Fillets and smaller fish can't be cut.
- **Fish Guts**: Gut Fish gives one from a whole fish over 0.6 weight, vanilla's filleting limit. They weigh 10% of the fish, which loses that weight. Smaller fish, fillets and salmon give none. Filleting later gives no more.
- **Fish Head**: Gut Fish takes the head off a whole fish over 1.0 weight (above the guts' 0.6, so small fish can't be farmed for heads): *Medium Walleye Head*, 10% of the weight and 6% of the food. It spoils like fresh fish (3 / 6 days), makes you sick raw, is a little unpleasant even cooked, goes into soups, stews or chum, and can't be dried. A small fish salted whole in the tub keeps its head (and its guts), as salted fish on the Volga were dried.
- **Fish Head Stock**: **Make Fish Head Stock** (Cooking, in hand) takes a Cooking Pot or Forged Pot holding 1.5 L of water and a Fish Head. Its first ingredient makes it **Fish Head Soup** or **Fish Head Stew** (two evolved recipes on the stock pot, vanilla's Soup and Stew templates, each with its own result pot), with **6 ingredients** plus seasonings: the head is listed but takes no slot. Named for what went in (*Fish Head Soup with Fish and Carrots*; two foods of one type by the type; past 3 kinds just *Fish Head Soup*). It carries the head's calories but not its hunger, keeps 3 / 5 days and gives the pot back. **Divide Food into 4 Bowls** / **2 Bowls**: Soup Bowls for a stock or soup, Stew Bowls for a stew, named after the dish (*Bowl of Fish Head Soup with Carrots*), at a table or counter.
- **Bone Broth**: **Make Bone Broth** (Cooking, in hand): a Pot or Forged Pot with 1.5 L of water and 2 Animal Bones, 1 Large Animal Bone or 6 Small Animal Bones (three recipes, one name). About 80 kcal, mostly protein (150 from a Large's marrow): kept small on purpose. The bones are kept and come back named *Boiled ...* (`modData.boiled`): every tool recipe still takes them, Make Bone Broth doesn't (`F.canBoil`), and vanilla's Smash Bone passes the mark to the pieces (`F.onSmashBone`, after the hand craft on TP Core's `Handcraft`; the vanilla recipe is not changed). It lists one bone-pile icon (`BoneBrothIngredient`), as Fish Head Stock lists its head. At 2 a pot a cow's 7-10 bones and 3-5 large give about 8 pots, a pig about 4. Becomes **Bone Broth Soup** or **Bone Broth Stew**.
- **Chicken Stock**: **Make Chicken Stock**: the same pot and 2 parts, Chicken Feet and chicken or turkey heads in any mix (vanilla turkeys give no feet). A foot gives 35 kcal (the gelatin), a head 15 (vanilla's heads have none), neither any hunger; the stock lists one chicken-foot icon (`ChickenStockIngredient`), never named, taking no slot (MaxItems 7). Becomes **Chicken Soup** or **Chicken Stew**.
- **Every stock, and its soup and stew, starts at hunger -5, unhappiness -10, boredom -10** (a design decision: a stock is worth its taste). Vanilla's `addItem` makes the soup or stew a fresh pot with the stock's food but no mood and no listed parts; `F.carryStock` adds the stock's unhappiness and boredom back and lists its parts first. Soup cooks 50 minutes, stew 70, the stocks 70.
- **Meat** goes to the salt or the pot as vanilla's butchering cuts it, no knife work: Beef and Venison 0.5 weight, Steak and Mutton Chop 0.3, Pork 0.5, Pork Chop 0.3.
- **Leg of Pork** (for country ham): an adult pig gives some of its Pork as whole legs, 2.5 weight each. Five of the Pork it just gave make one leg, which takes their food, so the pig's meat stays vanilla's; at most half its Pork goes, and three legs. On a Butchering Hook about one or two (two or three from a big pig), from the ground usually none. Piglets give none. Sandbox *Butchering gives legs of pork*.
- **Fat from butchering**: pigs give Pork Fat, cattle Beef Suet, sheep Mutton Fat (on a Butchering Hook about 5-7, 8-10 and 2-4 for an average animal; 1 or 2 on the ground). Young animals and deer give none. See *Fat* below.

### The Salting Tub

**Build** (Carpentry 2): 4 planks, 6 nails, 2 twine; hammer and saw kept. It holds 50 weight or 35 pieces, fish **or** meat: the first piece in decides. No crafting window: everything is on its right-click menu, and the **Salting Tub Info** window (from the menu) shows the stage, what is in it, the place and its temperature, the cure's bar and speed, and what a warm cure is taking. A tub with anything in it can't be picked up: put it where the cure should run before the first piece goes in.

1. **Put in.** **Put \<fish or meat\> in the Tub**, a row for each kind you carry: **All** (as many as fit), then **One**, oldest first. Fish goes in raw: whole up to 0.6 weight (vanilla's filleting limit: a small fish keeps its guts, easily ripped out once it is dry), a Gutted Fish, a Fish Half or a fillet. **A whole fish over 0.6 is gutted first** (Gut Fish), and a whole or gutted fish over 4.5 weight (about 2 kg) is too thick to salt whole: split it. Meat goes in as the butcher gives it: Beef, Steak, Mutton Chop, Venison, Pork, Pork Chop, a Leg of Pork, chicken and turkey cuts or whole birds, Rabbit Meat. Fresh, stale or part-eaten, not rotten, frozen or cooked: a refused row says why in red. Until the batch starts, **Unpack** gives everything back, aged as it would be lying out (Food Spoilage included): an unsalted tub is no cold store.
2. **Salt and press.** Three start rows, each in red with the reason when it can't start (the wrong kind of batch, a leg in a Corn batch, poultry in a Country Cure, not enough salt, no plank, no stone). Each salts the batch and lays a **Plank and a Flat Stone** on it (both come back with the last piece out). Salt goes by each piece's weight in the tub: 2 for each quarter weight, to the nearest and never under 2, about 15% (Volga salters use 10 to 15%): a small panfish 2, a half-weight bluegill 4, a 4.05 weight gutted walleye 32, a Beef, Venison or Pork 4, a Steak or chop 2; a Leg of Pork, poultry and rabbit half that, 1 a quarter weight (about 8%, a country ham's dry cure), 10 for a 2.5 leg. A vanilla fillet counts as the food it holds (9 times its raw weight). The salt must be on you (bags count), part-used first, through TP Core's `Uses` (a vanilla shaker holds 10, the Salt mod's Evaporated Salt 5 and a Bag of Salt 50; the Salt mod's empties come back). Cooking xp 3 a piece. The heaviest piece sets the time. **Cure Fish**: 1 day if all are up to 1.1 weight, 2 days with one of 2.2 or more, in between by weight; out comes **Salt Cured** fish. **Corn the Meat**, the short cure, for charque and hot smoking: 2 days for a 0.5 cut, by weight for lighter ones (about 29 hours for Steaks and Chops), never under a day; out comes **Corned** meat (*Steak (Corned)*). Not for a Leg of Pork: too thick. **Country Cure**, the long one, for the Smokehouse's dry cured meat and country ham: **10 days** for cuts, **21** if a Leg of Pork is in the batch; out comes **Country Cured** meat (*Leg of Pork (Country Cured)*). Not for poultry or rabbit. The clock runs while you're away (the ledger's totals; a look at the tub brings it up to now). **The cold slows the salt** (`F.TUB_CURE_POINTS`): full speed from 2 C, half at −5 C, a quarter from −10 C, by the tub's place (Core's temperature there: the open air and a roof the hour's, a room or a basement its own; ledger totals `F.TOTALS.tubCure`, the batch's `tubDone` and its marks).
3. **Take out.** **Take Out** (All, One) once cured. Salt Cured fish, Corned and Country Cured meat keep 7 / 14 days (Corned poultry and rabbit 3 / 6) and must be cooked to eat; the cure starts their clock afresh, less what a warm cure took, and pieces left waiting in the tub go off as they would lying out. The last piece gives the plank and stone back.
4. **Rinse, if you like.** **Rinse Off Salt** (a recipe in the piece's submenu, 0.15 L of clean carried water each, batches in the Craft window, works in the dark) or **Rinse at Sink** (a sink, bathtub, barrel or well of clean water within 3 tiles). Tainted water is refused. Rinsed, every later stage is less salty (two thirds of the thirst; Salt Dried fish also loses its unhappiness) and keeps a tenth less long. Skipping it changes nothing.

**A warm cure**: while it is above 20 C where the tub stands (TP Core's place temperature: the hour's air in the open or under a roof, a room's own mean indoors, the basement's), the flesh by the spine starts to spoil (bone taint) before the salt reaches it. The batch picks up spoilage that shortens the finished food's keeping: a day at 21 C about 14%, two days at 25 C about 38%. The Salting Tub window shows it while curing (*Spoiling (Warm cure)*). The long cure is a cold-season job, as country hams were salted from the first hard frost: 10 days in a warm season costs a cut most of its keeping, and 21 rots a leg. **The long cure wants the cold** (`F.LONG_CURE_ABOVE` 10, `F.LONG_CURE_ROT_AT` 15, ledger total `longCureSpoil`; the batch is marked `countryCure`): above **10 C** where the tub stands a Country Cure batch spoils in step with the warmth, rotten after a leg's 21 days at 15 C; at 12 C a leg loses 40% and cuts 19%, at 15 C cuts lose 48%. So dry cured meat and ham are an October-to-April craft, with no season lock. **A tub in a basement** goes by the basement's temperature instead (TP Core's ground mean, a month behind the season: ledger totals `warmCureBasement`, `longCureBasement`, `F.TOTALS.warmCure` / `longCure` at the Basement): no warm cure ever, and a leg's long cure loses about 4% in May, a third to two thirds June to September, 18% in October.

### The drying stations

**The Drying Frame, Hanging Beam, Gantry and the Smokehouse** have no crafting window: each piece hangs on its own,
with its own clock, and stays until taken. Right-click the rack (any of the Smokehouse's four tiles): a status line (pieces, kg of its total; hover for the place), **Info**,
a **Hang** submenu for each kind you carry (*Hang Salt Fish*, *Hang Gutted Fish*, *Hang Corned Meat*, *Hang Country
Cured Meat*, and in the Smokehouse *Hang Sausages*), each kind with **One** or **All** (lightest first, so the most fit; a kind the rack won't take is red,
its hover saying why: too heavy with every rack's limit, full, or the place), **Take Done (n)** (greyed until a piece
is done, its hover saying when the next one will be) and **Take Everything Off** (pieces not done come off as they
were hung, less the keeping the hanging spoiled: warmth, frost, wet, a muggy season, under-salting; hours it spent
drying don't age it, hours it hung without drying (a shut room, the wrong place) do, at the normal pace). **Info** is also at the bottom of the main menu. Hanging and taking down use the take-down-curtain
movement, one piece an action. A rack with anything on it can't be picked up.

The rack's window lists the place, the air, wind and wetness, what it holds, and a row a piece (its bar with the share
dried and time left, or why it stands still, or *Done* / *Rotten*), sorted furthest along or least along; with more than
one Hang group on it, a tab for each over the list (*All*, *Salt Fish (4)*, ...);
hover a row for its method, what it comes out as, the time left and any spoilage. Under the list, **Take Done (n)**
and **Take All (n)** buttons, as the menu's rows, and a **drop square**
takes a selection dragged from the inventory or a loot window: green when it hangs everything, orange when some will
be refused, red when none (the reason shows over you on release). A finished piece keeps on the rack as food does;
dry cured meat left hanging ages faster, as it does today.

A rack takes **salt fish** (Salt Cured), **gutted fish** and halves (cold drying) and **salted meat** (charque, or dry
curing in the shade). **Country Cured meat, pork too, hangs in the shade** (`F.meatRefusal`): on a **Frame, Beam or
Gantry under a roof**, or a **Frame or Beam in a room or a basement**, where it dry cures to air dried meat (below), or
in the Smokehouse; never in the open (sun and rain are charque's). **Corned pork and sausages hang in the Smokehouse
only.** A rack takes Corned beef, mutton and venison anywhere, for charque. A piece heavier than a rack's heaviest goes
on a bigger rack, or is split; a caught fish is at most about 20 kg, so the biggest are split to hang (halves of about
10 kg, onto the Gantry).

**What you see**: a rack's spots fill by weight (a spot each 2.5 kg on a frame, 15 on a Beam, 50 on a Gantry), shared
by kind, so a mixed rack shows each of salt fish, gutted fish and meat; a spot shows its least dry piece (fresh, half
dry, dry), rotten if any piece in it rotted.

The **Smokehouse Info** window is the rack's window with a **Fire** row (the joined Stone Hearth: none, out, banked or stoked) and each
piece's own smoke under its bar: a hot run counting, its cold smoke against its need (*too warm to count* with a fire
joined on a day over 15 C), and a link's days to stale.
Gutted fish doesn't hang there (cold drying needs moving air). While finished pieces wait inside, something hangs by
the door (TP Core's look).

### Salt Dried fish

**Hang Salt Fish** on a rack: 12 days at full speed (20 C or warmer in dry air). Salt Dried fish keeps 120 / 180 days (rinsed 108 / 162), is very salty but safe as it is, and goes straight into soup, stew, rice or pasta. **Soak Fish** (1 L of water) gives a mild fish that cooks like fresh and keeps 2 / 4 days, a darker brown than fresh fish as real soaked fish is. Packed and cured fish aren't safe yet: only drying makes salt fish safe uncooked.

### Charque

**Hang Corned Meat** on a rack: 5 days at full speed, in hot sun (25 C and up) and a breeze. It slows in cooler weather (two fifths at 10 C) and stops at freezing; still air dries at two thirds, a good breeze adds a fifth; rain on an open station takes back a tenth of the progress every 6 hours. About a week in a Kentucky summer under a roof. **Charque** (*Beef Charque*, *Mutton Charque*, *Venison Charque*) weighs about half the raw cut, keeps 90% of the food, keeps 120 / 180 days, and can't be eaten until **Soak Charque** (1 L of water) soaks it: *Beef Charque (Soaked)* is a little salty, cooks like a steak, goes into stews and pies, and keeps 2 / 4 days.

### Stockfish (the cold route)

1. **Gut Fish** (and **Split Fish** if needed).
2. **Hang Gutted Fish** on a frame, Beam or Gantry in the open, under an open roof or in a room with a window open (it needs moving air: in a shut room it waits, a basement and the Smokehouse refuse it). Frozen fish is fine. The hanging fish fade to grey.
3. 32 days at full speed: 0 to 5 C in a good breeze; about 50 to 55 days hung November to January.
4. **Mature**: stockfish matures in storage over 60 days on the fish's own food clock: any container works, a fridge slows it and a freezer stops it. Young it's plain *Walleye Stockfish*, matured *Walleye Stockfish (Matured)*, and the tooltip shows how far along it is. Stockfish spawned with no stamp (the debug Item List, loot) counts as ripe. A **Stockfish Pallet** (build menu: Carpentry 2, 3 planks, 6 nails, hammer and saw kept; a 20 weight container) speeds it where the warehouses kept stockfish: an aired room (TP Core's place: a window to the outside open, smashed or empty) ×2, 30 days, a shut room ×1.5, 40 days; covered, outdoors or in a basement no faster than a shelf. Right-click it to see its speed. It shows a stack of stockfish, a third, two thirds or full by weight, while it holds nothing else. A **Stockfish Bale**: right-click selected stockfish, *Tie a Bale*, with one use of Twine or a Rope. It holds 10 weight of stockfish and nothing else (weight reduction 30), the fish keep ripening inside, on a pallet too, and *Untie Bale* gives the fish and the cord back.
5. **Soak Fish** (1 L of water) before eating (*Walleye Stockfish (Soaked)*): stockfish is as hard as wood and keeps 365 / 540 days, enough to reach next winter's fish. The soaked fish keeps how ripe it was: unripe it's dull (unhappiness +10), halfway plain, ripe it cheers you up (-10), and a soup or stew takes its share.

### The Smokehouse, hot and cold smoking

A plank shed on four tiles, two by two, its pair of doors under the gable. The shed, its Stone Hearth and the flue are **TP Core's** (Core's README, "The Smokehouse"), so other TP mods can smoke in it too; this mod hangs its fish, meat and sausage there and steps only its own stacks. Inside it's always *In the Smokehouse* (TP Core's `Smokehouse.PLACE`: an aired room's temperature, the day's mean, never the grid's 22 C): no rain, no wind, salt fish at about two thirds of the open speed, meat dry curing, no stockfish. The two tiles in front of its doors stay clear, and nothing can be built there.

**Connect the Stone Hearth**: TP Core's **Stone Hearth** (Masonry 2: 12 Stone Blocks, 12 Clay, trowel kept) on a tile beside the shed joins it with a **clay pipe** (2 Clay Pipe Segments + 1 Clay, Mason's Trowel kept) or a **stone trench** (3 Flat Stones + 2 Clay, a shovel or spade kept). No skill, about half a minute, a little Masonry XP. On the Stone Hearth's menu and the shed's right-click. It may stand by either long side or the back, not at the door end and not with its own door towards the shed; one Stone Hearth a shed; the fire must be out. **Disconnect** gives the pipe or stones back (the clay is lost). Joined, neither picks up; the Stone Hearth's hole is lidded (cooking on it carries on) and TP Core's Forged Cauldron still fits. The Stone Hearth window says where its flue goes and what it does.

**Hot smoking**: every salt stack (Salt Cured fish, or salted meat) in a joined shed counts the Stone Hearth's **stoked hours in a row**. A lit Stone Hearth is stoked; Core's *Bank the Fire* rakes it down, *Stoke the Fire* brings it back. When a stack has enough, it finishes at once as **Hot Smoked Fish** or **Hot Smoked** meat (*Beef (Hot Smoked)*); **pork comes out as vanilla's Ham** with the cut's own weight and food, so Slice Ham and ham dishes take it; a stack with a **Leg of Pork never takes a cook** (its stoked hours are smoke): by the heaviest piece as it weighs cured, fish 4 hours (all up to 1.1 weight) to 6 (2.2) and on past it at the same slope (about 8 for a walleye caught at 4), meat 6 hours (0.3) to 8 (0.5), so a Steak cured to 0.27 about 5.7 and a Beef cured to 0.45 about 7.5; never under 4 nor over 12 (`F.hotHours`). Adding wood or banking it up to an hour keeps the run (the gap doesn't count); longer, the run is over, its hours become **smoke** (below) and the next stoking starts from nothing. A fire that burns out while you're away counts only until its wood ran out. Short of a cook, fish dries on as Sheltered and comes out Salt Dried; meat dry cures (below). The popup shows *Hot Smoke: x of y h stoked in a row*. While finished stacks wait inside, **a piece hangs by the door for every two** (a country ham, a smoked chicken, a smoked rabbit; TP Core's).

**Hot Smoked** fish and meat are cooked and ready to eat: 7 / 14 days, about 63% of the fresh fish's weight or 70% of the raw cut, all of its food, a little salt (thirst 15, rinsed 10), a small mood lift (unhappiness -5). Fish go into soups, stews, rice, pasta, sandwiches and salads; meat also into pies, tacos and burritos.

**Hot smoked chicken, turkey and rabbit**: vanilla's chicken and turkey legs, wings and fillets, a whole **Chicken** or **Turkey** and **Rabbit Meat** are brined and hot smoked, nothing else, as they were on the farm (lean bird fat goes rancid in the drying; raw poultry carries salmonella and wild rabbit tularemia, which only cooking kills). In the Salting Tub they take half red meat's salt (a whole chicken 5) and **Corn the Meat** brines them (Country Cure refuses them): a cut about 29 hours, a **whole chicken 38, a turkey 48**, since a hollow bird is no thicker than a big cut (it counts as weight 0.4 or 0.5, `F.TIMING_WEIGHTS`). Out come *Chicken Leg (Corned)* and the rest, keeping **3 / 6 days**. They hang in the **Smokehouse only**, apart from red meat, and there they never dry or take smoke: a stack waits for **its stoked hours in a row** (a cut about 5.7, a whole chicken 7, a turkey 8), a broken run starts again, and meanwhile it spoils as under-salted Corned meat does, about **2.3 times as fast** (rotten in about 12 days at 10 C, 2.4 at 20 C, and within **14 days** of hours above freezing (`F.HOT_ONLY_MAX_DAYS`, `F.hotOnlyUnderRate`; frozen, a bird keeps); a banked fire warms it). The popup reads *Hot smoking only* and *Waiting (Needs a stoked Stone Hearth)* until the Stone Hearth is stoked. Out comes **Hot Smoked** meat with the cut's name (*Turkey Wing (Hot Smoked)*, *Chicken (Whole) (Hot Smoked)*), Hot Smoked meat's values and vanilla's food. It isn't sliced; **Carve Chicken (Hot Smoked)** and **Carve Turkey (Hot Smoked)** (a knife, on a surface) cut a whole smoked bird into 2 legs, 2 wings and 2 fillets, as vanilla's Cut Chicken does a raw one: the bird's food shared by vanilla's cut hunger, 90% of its weight. A bird corned whole can be carved before it is smoked too: **Carve Chicken / Turkey (Corned)** gives the six cuts at the bird's stage, each keeping its age and its whole cure and spoilage record, so the cuts smoke on as the bird would, in a cut's shorter times.

**Cold smoking**: **smoke** = banked hours plus stoked runs that broke off before a cook, counted only on days of **15 C or less** (stoked or banked; a smoulder lifts the meat about 15 C, and over 30 C it cooks rather than cures). When a stack has dried, if it has had its **hours of smoke** (by the heaviest piece: fish 24 hours up to 1.1 weight, 48 from 2.2; meat 60 for a cut, 120 with a Leg of Pork; they needn't come in a row), it comes out **Cold Smoked Fish** or smoked dry cured meat, named by its animal: **Beef (Cold Smoked)** (beef and steak), **Mutton (Cold Smoked)**, **Venison (Cold Smoked)**, **Pork (Cold Smoked)**, and from a Leg of Pork **Smoked Country Ham** (every dry cured meat is *Animal (Cold Smoked)* or *Animal (Dry Cured)*, as the other stages; slices *Beef Slice (Dry Cured)*, aged *Beef (Cold Smoked, Aged)*). Short of that, fish is Salt Dried and meat is air dried (below). **Any hour of fire** dries the shed as if it were **15 C warmer banked, 20 C stoked** (`F.BANKED_LIFT`, `F.STOKED_LIFT`), with the humidity halved per 10 C of lift and the outdoor wind, and the popup's Drying Rate shows it. Salt fish leaves the 27-day floor (`MIN_DRY_RATE`) from about -2 C outdoors banked, -7 C stoked, and reaches full speed (12 days) from about 5 C banked, 0 C stoked; meat gains in the same way. The popup's *Smoke* line reads *x of y h of smoke*, or *Too warm to count* over 15 C. Both keep 120 / 180 days (rinsed 108 / 162), as Salt Dried and Charque, and are eaten as they are: thirst 25, a small mood lift (unhappiness -3); the same dishes as Hot Smoked.

### Fat: Lard, Tallow and Cracklings

**Put Pork Fat in a Pot** / **Put Suet in a Pot**: an empty Pot or Forged Pot and 2 raw pieces (suet and mutton fat mix). It cooks on any stove or fire in 60 minutes and burns at 150. **Pour Off Lard** / **Pour Off Tallow** leaves the pot holding 0.5 L of Lard (Melted) or Tallow (Melted). **Strain Lard** / **Strain Tallow** takes 0.5 L from any container through a Cheese Cloth, Dish Towel, Rag, Sheet or Strainer (kept): vanilla's Lard or the new **Tallow** (Lard's values, bakes wherever Lard does) and one **Cracklings** (7 / 14 days; salads, soups, stews, sandwiches). A burnt pot only takes **Scrape Out Burnt Fat**, which returns the pot. Raw fat keeps 2 / 4 days and is dangerous uncooked; Lard, Tallow and melted fat don't spoil. Two pieces make one block, so a pig's Lard (2 or 3 blocks on the Hook) is worth about its meat.

**In the Forged Cauldron**: **Put Fat in the Cauldron** (on the menu of a Stone Hearth with TP Core's cauldron fitted) puts one piece (the nearest to going off) or all of a kind you carry in, 0.25 L a piece, up to 240. Kept steaming (85 C or more) for an hour it becomes Lard (Melted) or Tallow, which **Fill from the Cauldron** puts in any container; strain it as above. The cauldron never burns fat. Raw fat left in a cold cauldron goes rancid when its oldest piece would have rotted (Empty the Cauldron takes it).

### Potted Meat

1. **Put Meat in Lard or Tallow** (an empty Pot or Forged Pot, a block of Lard or Tallow, one cut, 1 Salt) or **Put Meat in the Melted Fat** (a pot holding 0.5 L of Lard (Melted) or Tallow, one block's worth): a **Pot of Meat in Fat**.
2. **Add Meat to the Pot**: up to four cuts in all (Beef, Steak, Mutton Chop, Venison, Pork, Pork Chop, and vanilla's chicken and turkey legs, wings and fillets and Rabbit Meat, in any mix), uncooked only. Poultry and rabbit otherwise only hot smoke (below): no charque, dry curing or cold smoke.
3. Cook it on any stove or fire: 120 minutes, burns at 240.
4. **Pack Potted Meat** into an empty Ceramic Jar, Glazed Ceramic Jar or Ceramic Pot: the pot back and a **Crock of Potted Meat**, one portion a cut (its food plus a quarter block of fat, about 1,000 calories), eaten like any food (its name stays plain; it weighs less as it empties), the vessel back with the last bite. Cooking XP 3 a cut.

The crock **keeps by place**: about 120 / 180 days in a powered fridge or at 4 C, longer as it gets colder and stopped once frozen (−2 C and below: `F.POTTED_AGE_CURVE`), about 110 / 165 in a winter basement and 64 / 96 in a summer one (5 to 13 C, a month behind the season), in a room by its temperature (TP Core's: a shut pantry follows the last three days and is stuffy in July, about 17 / 25 at 23 C, 14 / 21 at 25 C and over; 18 / 27 at 22 C while the grid is on), stopped in a powered freezer. The first portion taken costs a third of the days it had left, once. A part-used Lard gives only what is left of it.

Vanilla bleeds a carcass into any container; here that blood is food and goes off.

## Seasons

Drying usually takes 12 to 55 days, and the time of year decides how a batch goes. Weather is recorded every 10 in-game minutes for the whole world, so stations keep drying while you are away.

- **Salt drying** takes 12 days at full speed, 20 C or above in dry air. Cold slows it (a quarter speed at 10 C) and so does air above 50% humidity, but it never drops below about 44% speed, so frost slows it without stopping it.
- **Charque** takes 5 days at full speed, from 25 C, scaled down to nothing at 0 C, times the same damp-air factor and a wind factor (0.65 still, 1.2 in a good breeze). No minimum rate and no heat damage.
- **Cold drying** takes 32 days at full speed: 0 to 5 C in a good breeze. Frost slows it (80% at -5 C, 60% below), so does warmth toward 10 C (67%), and it stops above 10 C. Wind sets the rest: 50% in still air, up to 120% at a wind intensity of 0.25. Live wind in a dry spell averages about 0.11, so a batch hung November to January takes about 50 to 55 days (a gameplay compromise: real stockfish hangs about three months).
- **Fresh fish is fragile**: until a cold stack is 30% dried, hard frost below -5 C (a tenth of its keeping per 72 hours) and lying wet above 0 C (5% per 48 hours) add to its spoilage, scaled by how wet it still is, together never past a quarter. Rot from a warm season is scaled the same way.
- **Rain**: open stations stop until it ends and the wet has dried off; progress is paused, not lost, except charque's tenth per 6 hours. Snow counts as rain for salt fish but doesn't bother cold drying. Cold fish waits to dry off after rain too.
- **The places**, read from the station's square (inside the Smokehouse a piece carries TP Core's `Smokehouse.PLACE`, *In the Smokehouse*, set by `F.rackPlace`) (Core's `Weather.placeOf(station)`):
  - **Open**: no roof overhead.
  - **Covered**: a roof overhead but not a room (a porch, an open shed, a floor above with no walls round it). Rain can't reach it. Salt fish and charque dry at the open rate in the shade, by Core's covered temperature (the hour's air, as the open); dry curing too (`dryCureCovered` / `underCureCovered`); cold drying at 75% of the wind (`F.COVERED_COLD_RATE`; its hard frost: `hardFrostCovered`). A tub under a roof goes by the same air.
  - **Aired room**: a room with a window to the outside open, smashed, without its glass or an empty frame, and not barricaded (Core's `AiredRoom`, the day's mean, at most 1 C over in winter). It dries as a roof: salt fish and charque at the open rate (charque with the wind), dry curing (`aired`, `charqueAired`, `dryCureAired`), cold drying at 60% of the wind (`F.AIRED_COLD_RATE`, `coldAired`, `hardFrostAired`). A tub there minds the room (`warmCureAired`, `longCureAired`, `tubCureAired`).
  - **Shut room** (Indoors): a room with every window to the outside shut or barricaded. **Nothing dries by air there**: salt fish, charque, dry curing and cold drying wait for a window (*Stopped (Shut room: open a window)*, `F.shut`). A tub, Corned meat's spoiling and unsalted fish's rot go by Core's room temperature (the three-day mean, 22 C while the grid is on: `underCureRoom`, `warmCureRoom`, `longCureRoom`, `rawSpoilRoom`). Gutted fish may hang there, waiting. In a room every meat stack dry cures (`F.dryCuring` reads `F.sheltered`, still air); in an aired room only a Country Cured stack does, as under a roof.
  - **The Smokehouse** (a piece keeps TP Core's word in its `state.place`, and `F.TOTALS` maps it to the ledger's Shed): an aired room's air (Core's `Smokehouse.AIR`, the day's mean, never the grid's 22 C) with still air (`sheltered`, `charqueSheltered`, `dryCure`, `underCure`, `hotOnlyUnder`), plus its fire on the day's air; Corned meat in the open spoils by the hour's (`underCureOpen`); meat there dry cures and is never charque. Gutted fish is refused.
  - **Basement**: still air, by the basement's own temperature (TP Core: about 5 C January to March, 13 C July to September, a month behind the season): salt fish at 65% of that (`basementSheltered`, at the 27-day floor all year), charque likewise (`charqueBasement`), dry curing at two thirds of `F.dryCureRate` (`dryCureBasement`): a cellar is where hams cured. Gutted fish is refused. The stack popup shows the place as Basement.

Spoilage goes by the place's temperature (Core's), as the drying does.

- **Gutted fish** spoils above 10 C: it rots on the frame after about 2 days at 10 C, sooner the warmer it gets. The open air and a roof by the hour's air (`rawSpoil`), a shut room, an aired room and a basement by their own (`rawSpoilRoom`, `rawSpoilAired`, `rawSpoilBasement`). The rot is weighted by the stack's wetness **squared** (the skin: half dry, a quarter of the harm), so stockfish is a job for November to January: February is a gamble, October a loss, and a roof or an open window, drying slower, rots more in a warm spell than the open air (real stockfish hung on open racks).
- **Salt fish and charque** suffer in a muggy season (above 22 C and the humidity above 40%) **only in still air**: each day of that takes a twentieth of the finished food's keeping. In the Smokehouse by the season's mean (`saltSpoil`: mould and rancid fat come of days of damp warmth), in a shut room and a basement by their own (`saltSpoilRoom`, `saltSpoilBasement`): a stuffy July shut room (about 23 C) spoils a stack in weeks, a cellar never. The open air, a roof and an aired room keep the skin dry (`F.muggy`). **Dry curing is exempt** (country hams age through the summer heat).
- **Hot sun** (above 25 C) on an open salt fish stack under half dried hardens the skin over wet flesh: each 24 hours takes 5% off the keeping, scaled by how wet it still is, within the same quarter. A covered or indoor station is in the shade. On the forecasts that costs an open stack 2 to 5% from May to August. Charque takes no harm from the sun.
- **A warm cure**: see the Salting Tub. A 2-day cure under a roof lost 19% in July, 14% in June and 10% in August here.

**Hot smoking in detail.** Core's `Fire.logFire` keeps each Stone Hearth's record in its fire state (`tpFire`): `stokedHours`, `bankedHours`, and the stoked session running (`session`, `sessionBase`, `stokedLast`; 1 h grace, `Fire.SESSION_GRACE`), counted only up to `fireEnds` because vanilla burns a lit fire's away time in one go on load. A salt fish or salted meat piece gets `hotSmokeHoursNeeded` when hung, from its weight (`F.HOT_FISH_HOURS` 1.1 → 4, 2.2 → 6; `F.HOT_MEAT_HOURS` 0.3 → 6, 0.5 → 8; clamp 4-12) and keeps `hotSmokeHours`, `coldSmokeHours`, `smokeFrom` (the Stone Hearth's `fireId`), `smokeSession`, `lastStoked`, `lastBanked`. The World (and the Smokehouse window when it looks) steps it against the joined Stone Hearth (`F.updateRack`); stoked hours in a session that has ended (or that started and ended between looks) go to `coldSmokeHours`, as do banked hours. A done cook marks the piece `cooked` and done; taken off, `F.rackOutput` makes a `HotSmokedFish` / `HotSmokedMeat` (`F.smokedItem`) carried from it.

**Dry curing**: meat hung in the Smokehouse **never comes out Charque**; charque is the frames'. It dries on its own need by the heaviest piece, **3 weeks at full rate for a Beef, 6 for a Leg of Pork**, in cool air: full rate from **12 C**, down to **a quarter** in frost, slowed by damp air as salt fish, no wind; the fire's warmth as above. A cut hung in December with a fire a few hours a day takes about 7 weeks and a leg about 3 months; with no fire, 12 weeks and more in frost. With its smoke it is Beef (Cold Smoked) and the others above; without, **air dried**: **Beef (Dry Cured)**, **Mutton / Venison / Pork (Dry Cured)**, and from a leg **Country Ham**. A Country Ham (smoked or not) is its own item and keeps **180 / 365 days**. **Corned meat is under-salted** for the long hang: until a hot cook is done it spoils by the shed's temperature, the fire's lift included (rotten in 4 weeks at 10 C, five times faster at 20 C; above freezing never slower than **6 weeks**, `F.UNDER_CURE_MAX_DAYS`, while frozen hours keep the slow curve, five times slower at 0 C; a rinsed piece 1 / 0.9 as fast, `F.underLoss`; popup line *Under-salted*); Country Cured pieces in the same stack don't. **A stack that rots on any station comes down as what was hung**, rotten (*Corned Beef (Rotten)*, a pressed or gutted fish), not as the finished thing. **It looks rotten** (third tile sheet `_03`, `F.FIRST_ROTTEN_LOOK`, `F.rottenLook`, `F.stackRotten`): one look a kind (salt fish, cold dried fish, meat) on the Frame, Beam and Gantry; a running stack when it is lost as a whole (a mixed stack with Country Cured pieces dries on and keeps its look), a finished one when any piece in it rotted. Rotted pieces keep their `stackId` and `style`, so a mixed stack counts as one.

**Dry curing outside the shed**: a piece of Country Cured meat (its state's `longStack`) on a Frame, Beam or Gantry dry cures by where it hangs (`F.curePlace`): under a roof or in an aired room at the shed's rate with the fire out, but by Core's temperature for the place (`dryCureCovered`, `dryCureAired`; slowed by damp air); in a **shut room** it waits for a window; in a **basement**, the curing cellar, by the basement's own temperature times two thirds (`F.dryCureRate(basement) x F.DRY_CURE_BASEMENT_RATE`, totals `dryCureBasement` / `underCureBasement`: a cut **9 to 11 weeks** hung December to February, **32 days** June to October, a leg 17 to 19 weeks and 9; under-salted meat there spoils by the same temperature); if its roof goes, it waits (*Stopped (Needs a roof)*). **In a shut room or a basement every meat stack dry cures** (`F.dryCuring` reads `F.sheltered`, still air): Corned meat there is under-salted as in the shed, and never Charque; under an open roof, in an aired room or in the open an all-Corned stack is still Charque. **The Hanging Beam and the Gantry may be built in a room or a basement** (`F.doorFree`; `Tiles.DOOR_KEPT` keeps both off a door's front). The Salting Tub goes by Core's temperature where it stands: the hour's air in the open or under a roof, the room's in a shut or aired room (22 C on the grid, so a press there warm cures), the basement's in a basement. Always air dried: Beef / Mutton / Venison / Pork (Dry Cured), Country Ham. Smoke, the hot cook and the fire's warmth stay the Smokehouse's. The World swaps the outputs through `F.stackStations`, and the stack waits at `F.SHED_WAIT` until it has been seen.

**Ageing and slices**. Dry Cured meat and the Country Ham **age** after they have dried, on the item's own food clock from the age it came down with (`modData.agedFrom`, set in `F.smokedItem`; one spawned without it, from the debug Item List or loot, counts all its days): a cut `F.AGE_CUT_DAYS` (30), a ham `F.AGE_HAM_DAYS` (90). `F.agedness` gives 0 to 1; `F.rename` turns it "(Aged)" once (`modData.aged`, kept), sets the tooltip by the tenth, and `F.applyAged` gives what is left of the piece (`F.shareLeft`) the aged mood: a cut Unhappiness -3 to **-8**, a ham -10 to **-40** with **Boredom -20**. That, with its slices, is the ham's reward; its calories are the Pork's. **Left hanging** where it dried (a done piece on a rack or in the Smokehouse) a piece ages in `F.HANG_AGE_SHARE` (three quarters) of the time: taken off, `F.rackOutput` moves `agedFrom` back by the extra days since it was done. A finished item can't be hung again (the racks take only what is still to dry), so the faster ageing ends there. Edible young; Hot Smoked meat, vanilla Ham and Charque never age.
**Slices** (`shared/TraditionalPreservationFishMeatSlices.lua`), on a surface with a knife as vanilla's Slice Ham: **Slice Dry Cured Meat** and **Slice Hot Smoked Meat** give a cut's worth of slices of about hunger 14 (`F.SLICE_HUNGER`, `F.sliceCount`: a Beef (Hot Smoked) 6, a Steak 3, a dry cured Beef 4, never under 2; the script makes two and OnCreate hands the character the rest; vanilla's Ham Slice is hunger 10); **Carve Country Ham** takes 5 slices (hunger 70 in all, `F.CARVE_HUNGER`) off a ham that is **kept** (`mode:keep`; `F.carveOff` lowers its food, mood and weight and leaves BaseHunger, as eating does); with 105 or less left on it (`F.CARVE_LAST`) only **Carve the Last of the Country Ham** shows, which takes it all. A 2.5 leg's ham (hunger 210): three carvings, 15 slices. `F.fillSlices` gives each slice its share of the piece's food, thirst and mood, the cut it came from (`modData.cut`, for "Beef Slice (Cold Smoked)", "Smoked Country Ham Slice (Aged)"), `aged` and `airDried`, and an age as far through its days as the piece was (`F.sliceAge`). Slices keep half the piece's days (their scripts: 60 / 90, 90 / 180, 3 / 7), use vanilla's Ham Slices icon and model, and go into the same dishes (`EvolvedRecipeName.json` names them there). Hot smoked pork is vanilla's Ham, which vanilla slices.

**The cook on the piece**: while a stoked run counts (`F.cookShare`: `hotSmokeHours / hotSmokeHoursNeeded`, nil with no run or a leg) the piece's bar shows the cook when it is ahead of the drying (`F.stackProgress`, capped at `F.SHED_WAIT`), and its row in the Smokehouse window says so under the bar. The cook done, `F.updateRack` marks the piece `cooked` and done. A broken run zeroes `hotSmokeHours`, so the bar falls back.

**Dry curing in detail.** `F.startSmoke` gives a meat piece `cureNeed` (`F.dryCureHours`), `smokeNeed` (`F.smokeHours`), `noHotSmoke` (a leg in it), `underCured` (a piece without `longCure`) and `allUnder` (every piece). In the shed (`F.dryCuring`: sheltered, the Smokehouse included, and `cureNeed`) `F.charqueStep` adds the ledger's `dryCure` total and, under-cured, `underCure` to `underSpoiled`; `F.smokeStep` adds the fire's gains from `dryCureBanked` / `dryCureStoked` and `underCureBanked` / `underCureStoked`, as it does for fish. `F.smokedItem` makes each piece: vanilla Ham (`F.carryHam`), `CountryHam` for a leg, `modData.airDried` for the name and tooltip, and `F.rottenStage` for a piece that rotted (as `F.rackOutput` does for any rotted piece).

**Cold smoking in detail.** The ledger keeps `clock` (all hours) and `smokeCool` (hours at or under `F.COLD_SMOKE_MAX`, 15 C). Each look, a piece (`F.smokeStep(state, fire, ledger)`) takes the cool share of the hours since its last look (`lastClock`, `lastSmokeCool`) and counts only that share of new banked hours and of stoked hours into smoke; `hotSmokeCoolHours` is the cool part of `hotSmokeHours`, which is what moves to `coldSmokeHours` when a session breaks. The same look adds the fire's warmth: banked hours times the ledger's (banked − Sheltered) drying per hour over the interval, stoked hours times (stoked − Sheltered) (totals `smokeBanked`/`smokeStoked` or `dryCureBanked`/`dryCureStoked` by `smokeMeat`, from `F.liftedRates`; `lastDraughtSheltered`, `lastDraughtBanked`, `lastDraughtStoked`) to `dried`, and sets `burning` to the fire's mode (Core's `Fire.STOKED` / `BANKED`) for the popup's Drying Rate. A piece's first look starts its warm totals. Each look sets `state.place` (`Smokehouse.PLACE` in the shed); taken off dried, `F.rackOutput` checks `F.coldDone` (`coldSmokeHours` ≥ the piece's `smokeNeed`) and makes `ColdSmokedFish` / `DryCuredMeat`. `F.hangPiece` starts the state from nothing and marks every total (`F.markStack`).

**The Smokehouse in detail.** The chamber is TP Core's (`entity_Smokehouse_Chamber.txt`, `Smokehouse.lua`, its World and menu: Core's README). This mod's World tracks its tiles too (Core's `Tiles.CHAMBERS`) and steps its piece record from the back left tile alone (`Smokehouse.recordTile`), always at `Smokehouse.PLACE`; its limits are `F.RACK_LIMITS.chamber` (90 kg, 150 pieces, 12 kg the heaviest). Its finished pieces hang by the door through Core's `Smokehouse.countsFinished`; a take redraws the shed (`Smokehouse.redraw`). Another mod hanging in the same shed keeps its own record. Tiles 856-869 (the chamber, its flue entries and the ham before the move) are left empty, so the Salting Tub's meat overlays stay at 870-875 (`Tiles.TUB_MEAT`: the last cut put in, or a leg whenever one is in).

### Tuning

Times, capacity, stage values, weather rates and spoilage limits are at the top of `42/media/lua/shared/TraditionalPreservationFishMeat.lua`. `PRESS_HOURS` (the longest press; `PRESS_MIN_HOURS` and the two `PRESS_..._WEIGHT` values set the rest), `DRY_HOURS`, `COLD_DRY_HOURS` and `CHARQUE_HOURS` are the drying times at a rate of 1 (the sandbox options set them when the game starts). Restart the game after changing them. Hot smoking's hours and cold smoking's share are at the end of `...Smokehouse.lua`; `COLD_SMOKE_MAX` is in the main file. Each rack's and the Smokehouse's room is `F.RACK_LIMITS` in `...Rack.lua`; the tub's `TUB_CAPACITY` and piece count in the main file.

## Known prototype caveats

- **Not fully tested in play.** Building, the station windows and sprites, charque, Potted Meat, the Smokehouse, hot and cold smoking have been seen in game. Seasons, spoilage and the sandbox options haven't been played through.
- A hanging piece's hover popup is the mod's own, built from the same rules. Its time left assumes the weather holds. The popup's layout is copied from vanilla's slot popup, so compare the two after a game update.
- A room is warmed only by the power grid (22 C, as vanilla keeps it); a generator or a fire in the room doesn't warm a drying station or a tub (only the Smokehouse's own Stone Hearth warms it).
- Vanilla's cutting can also give a roe sac in season. Gutting and packing only give guts, and gutting a head.
- Vanilla's Divide Food into Bowls only knows vanilla's pots, so the stock has its own two copies of it. Compare them with vanilla's after a game update.
- In a dish's "Contains" list, a fish or meat stage shows one icon for that stage, not its species or cut: the list records a stand-in item (`...Ingredient`). Dish names are unaffected.
- Every Fish Head shares one icon, a largemouth bass's head, and lies on the ground as that icon at half size.
- The season figures come from the game's forecasts for default weather, not from a played year. Sandbox temperature settings will shift them.
- A fish over 4.5 weight has to be gutted and split before it goes in the tub; a piece over 12 kg won't fit on a Gantry, so split it to hang it.
- The tub holds 35 pieces at most, so fillets under 0.1 weight fill it by count before weight; a frame takes 200 pieces.
- A piece's hanging look is picked at random, so it may not match the fish. The beams only hang walleye and sauger shapes.
- On the ground most fish stages are the vanilla model of that species with a repainted texture (`WorldStaticModelsByIndex`); Hot and Cold Smoked fish use Salt Dried fish's models. Charque, salted, smoked, Dry Cured and rehydrated meat lie on the ground as the vanilla model of their cut, untinted.
- The tiledef number 5129 must be unique among your active mods. A tileset holds 512 tiles at most, and going over drops the whole tile file and every placed station, so the beams and the Smokehouse are in a second tileset.
- Multiplayer is not supported. This mod is singleplayer only for now.
